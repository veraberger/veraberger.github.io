## Tidal disruption events

Fundamental timescales relevant to black hole accretion -- dynamical, thermal, viscous -- should scale linearly with the black hole mass. Through X-ray binary systems of stellar-mass black holes in the Milky Way, changes in accretion state over weeks to months have been extremely well-characterized in X-rays. We expect these same changes to happen over thousands to millions of years (far beyond human-observable timescales) for the supermassive black holes (SMBHs) at centers of galaxies, whose accretion histories are tied to galaxy formation and growth. 

A class of transients called tidal disruption events, where a star is torn apart by a supermassive black hole, allow us to watch an accretion disk form and evolve around an SMBH in real time. I am particularly interested in TDEs that undergo state transitions, and how we can use their observational properties to measure the mass of the supermassive black hole.
